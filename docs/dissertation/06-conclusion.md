# Chapter 6: Conclusions and Recommendations

> **Outline only** — awaiting results.

## 6.1 Conclusions

One conclusion per specific objective, each stated as what the evidence
supports and no more:

- **Objective I** — settlement identification and clustering.
- **Objective II** — settlement-level demand.
- **Objective III** — comparative economics of the three technologies.
- **Objective IV** — the optimum pathway per settlement from both lenses.

Then the general objective: whether a GIS-aided techno-economic and financial
framework can determine least-cost and investment-feasible pathways in a
heterogeneous county — and under what conditions the answer holds.

## 6.2 Recommendations

**For the Kajiado County Government.** The prioritised plan, the capital it
implies, and the reallocation it recommends against current practice.

**For REREC and KPLC.** Scheme ranking criteria; the treatment of the connection
fee; where the evidence says public capital is genuinely required rather than
conventionally assumed.

**For private developers.** The bankable pipeline; the parameters their case is
most sensitive to; what would need to change for the remainder to clear a
commercial hurdle.

**For national policy.** What county-level evidence implies for KNES targets and
for the design of results-based finance instruments.

## 6.3 Recommendations for further research

- Least-cost path MV routing in place of straight-line distance — the most
  consequential simplification remaining in the model.
- Demand-growth and productive-use trajectories, replacing static tiers.
- Reliability and quality of supply, so a grid connection and a mini-grid
  connection are not treated as equivalent service.
- Application to other Kenyan ASAL counties, testing the claim that the method
  generalises even where the results do not.
- Contribution of the footprint-based clustering and dispersed-settlement
  treatment back to the OnSSET community.

## 6.4 Closing

The framework's purpose is to make the invisible visible: to ensure that a
dispersed *manyatta* appears in a county investment decision as a costed,
ranked, financeable option rather than as noise in a dataset. Whether it achieves
that is a question about what the results show and about how honestly their
limits are reported.
