## 5.1 Interpretation against the research questions

Each question answered in turn, with the evidence and its limits.

## 5.2 The crossover, and what it means for county planning

The grid/off-grid crossover distance by settlement size is the practical output.
Discussion should address: how it compares to the implicit thresholds in current
scheme-approval practice; whether settlements currently queued for grid extension
sit on the wrong side of it; and what a reallocation would cost and save.

## 5.3 Dispersed settlements

What share of Kajiado's unelectrified population lives in settlements that a
1 km-resolution model would not resolve at all? This is the quantified answer to
the "invisible to budget cycles" claim in Section 1.5, and the clearest statement
of what building-footprint resolution buys.

Discussion must be honest about the decision-reason split: settlements decided by
the dispersed override were decided by assumption, and a plan in which that share
is large is a plan resting on the override's validity.

## 5.4 The connection fee

Section 3.5.4 singles out high household connection fees as a decisive barrier
even where the network passes nearby. With the fee modelled as its own term,
this chapter can quantify: its share of grid CAPEX; how many settlements flip
technology when it is subsidised or shifted; and what a subsidy programme
targeted at the fee alone would deliver per dollar compared with the same money
spent on line extension.

## 5.5 Comparison with the literature

Against OnSSET Kenya applications, KNES, and the Narok footprint-based study
(Ronoh & WRI, 2025) — the closest published analogue. Where results differ,
attribute the difference to its cause: resolution, the dispersed treatment, the
dual-perspective appraisal, or parameterisation. **Do not tune parameters until
the numbers agree**; the differences are the contribution, and explaining them is
the argument.

## 5.6 Implications for each user

- **Kajiado County Government** — the investment sequence, and what it costs to
  deviate from it.
- **REREC and KPLC** — scheme ranking, connection-fee policy, and where public
  money is genuinely necessary rather than merely conventional.
- **Private developers** — the bankable pipeline, and what would have to change
  for the rest to become bankable.
- **National policy** — what county-level evidence implies for KNES targets and
  for the design of results-based finance.

## 5.7 Limitations of this study

Restate the limitations of Section 1.7.1 with the actual measured magnitudes
rather than the anticipated ones: the structure-filter error as measured (V1);
the served-status bracket as measured (V2); the clustering stability as measured
(V3); the straight-line MV distance bias; and static demand.

Add what was learned in implementation: the sensitivity of results to ε, the
dependence of the mini-grid case on battery autonomy and storage cost, and the
degree to which developer viability rests on the assumed mini-grid tariff.

## 5.8 Contribution

Four claims, to be defended against what the results actually support:
building-footprint resolution for sub-national planning; explicit retention and
testing of dispersed settlements; dual-perspective appraisal with the connection
fee as a first-class term; and a reproducible, equation-traceable, openly
auditable implementation.
